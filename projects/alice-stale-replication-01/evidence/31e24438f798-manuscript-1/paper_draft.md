# Sentence- versus Turn-Level Retrieval under a 4096-Byte Budget: A Pre-Registered Replication with a Negative Primary Contrast

> Unreviewed model draft. Check every claim and add verified sources, methods and tables before submission.

## Abstract

We report a pre-registered replication comparing sentence-level and turn-level retrieval over 400 conversational scenarios under a 4096-byte evidence budget (54,000 retrieval rows). The primary contrast, sentence_bm25 minus turn_bm25 on the new-evidence inclusion outcome, was negative: mean -0.121, 95% CI [-0.146, -0.096], n=400; the interval excludes zero, so the sentence-level treatment performed significantly worse than the turn-level control. Per-arm results corroborate (sentence_bm25 'new' 0.174 vs turn_bm25 0.295; best 'new' was turn_timecue at 0.350). Sentence-level arms led only on update_session (sentence_timecue 0.663), so no method dominated. Reader evidence was uninformative: 16 graded responses, four per cell, with 95% CIs spanning ±0.75. The oracle intervention had zero scheduled pairs (null results), and the new-evidence/pass-rate association was unidentifiable (zero responses with new evidence present). Two anomalies—turn_rrf old-inclusion collapse (0.005) and turn_stop budget underfill—require investigation. The dataset was previously exposed; findings are confirmatory, with no novelty or deployable benefit claimed.

## Introduction

Systems that ground responses in prior conversational content must select evidence under tight context budgets. This study compares sentence-level and turn-level retrieval units, alongside density-, reciprocal-rank-fusion-, stop-word-, and time-cue-based variants, using a fixed 4096-byte evidence budget across 400 scenarios (54,000 retrieval rows; nine arms, n=400 per arm). Outcomes were lexical-inclusion metrics—'new', 'old', 'token_new', and 'update_session'—computed with punctuation-insensitive matching, plus retrieved byte counts. A small reader substudy asked two models (deepseek, kimi) to answer questions given evidence retrieved by the two BM25 arms, with opposite-model grading on a single dimension; 16 responses were attempted, answered, and graded. Two diagnostic components were pre-specified: an oracle update-versus-sham intervention and an association between new-evidence presence and reader pass rate. Per the artifact, this is a STALE replication on previously exposed data: methods, contrasts, and the reader sample were fixed before outcomes were examined, no winner selection was performed, and no unseen test set exists. The primary pre-registered contrast compares sentence_bm25 against the turn_bm25 control on the primary inclusion outcome; all other arms and analyses are secondary or diagnostic. Ceiling measurements show user_exact=387/400 and user_token=396/400, but assistant_exact=0/400 and assistant_only=0/400, meaning reference answers never appear lexically in assistant turns and inclusion metrics are measurable only on user content. We report results strictly as confirmatory evidence, preserving null and negative findings, and claim no novelty.

## Discussion

The primary contrast was negative and its 95% confidence interval excludes zero: sentence_bm25 underperformed turn_bm25 by 0.121 (CI95 [-0.146, -0.096], n=400) on the primary inclusion outcome. Per-arm results at the 4096-byte budget corroborate this direction: sentence_bm25 attained 'new' inclusion of 0.174 versus 0.295 for turn_bm25, and the highest 'new' value overall was turn_timecue at 0.350. Sentence-level arms led only on update_session (sentence_timecue 0.663; sentence_bm25 0.646 versus turn_bm25 0.584), but this coexists with worse 'new' inclusion, so no method dominates across metrics. Retrieval measurements were complete (bytes approximately 4083 of 4096) with tight intervals (about ±0.02). Two anomalies caution interpretation: turn_rrf's 'old' inclusion collapsed to 0.005 versus 0.27–0.49 in all other arms, suggesting degenerate fusion or a defect rather than a genuine effect; and turn_stop filled only 3917.8 bytes, underfilling the budget and biasing its comparison. Reader evidence is the weak link. With four responses per cell, pass rates (deepseek: 0.25 under both arms; kimi: 0.00 under sentence_bm25, 0.25 under turn_bm25) and paired contrasts (deepseek 0.00, CI95 [-0.75, 0.75]; kimi -0.25, CI95 [-0.75, 0.00]) are uninformative. The oracle update-versus-sham comparison had zero scheduled pairs (n=0, null means): no oracle evidence exists. The association analysis is unidentifiable because no response had new evidence present (new_present=1: zero responses for both models); the artifact correctly flags causal=false. Because assistant_exact=0/400, inclusion metrics capture only user-turn lexical overlap and cannot demonstrate semantic answer support. Consequently we draw no conclusions from the empty oracle or association analyses; recommend investigating turn_rrf and turn_stop before interpreting those arms; note that any reader-level claim requires a pre-fixed, far larger reader sample with human validation; recommend adding semantic-correctness evaluation plus at least one dense retriever or learned-reranker baseline; and note that novelty claims require a genuinely unseen test set. The two artifact SHA-256 hashes should be verified before citing results.

## Limitations

The artifact itself lists: (i) this is a STALE replication with previously exposed data, with no unseen test set and no new discovery; (ii) methods, contrasts, and reader sample were fixed before outcomes, with no winner selection; (iii) opposite-model paired grading is fallible, not human validation, and covers only a single dimension; (iv) the oracle intervention uses gold answers only for diagnostic construction, appends evidence, and is not a deployable retrieval method; (v) punctuation-insensitive lexical inclusion is not semantic entailment; and (vi) one dataset was used, with no dense pretrained retriever or learned reranker evaluated. Further limitations follow from the results. The reader substudy is severely underpowered (16 graded responses, four per cell; paired CIs span ±0.75), so no reader-level inference is supported. The oracle comparison is empty (zero scheduled pairs) and the evidence-presence/pass-rate association is unidentifiable (zero responses with new_present=1). The turn_rrf 'old'-inclusion collapse (0.005) and turn_stop budget underfill (3917.8 of 4096 bytes) are unexplained anomalies that bias those arms. Ceiling measurements (assistant_exact=0/400) bound what the inclusion metrics can show: answer content never appears lexically in assistant turns, so 'new'/'old' inclusion reflects only user-content overlap. All conclusions are restricted to this dataset, these lexical metrics, this budget, and the pre-registered contrasts; no generalization, novelty, or deployable benefit can be claimed.
